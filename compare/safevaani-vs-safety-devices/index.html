SafeVaani vs wearable safety devices

A device is one more thing to charge, carry and remember. Sometimes that is worth it. Here is when.

The short answer

A wearable wins when the trigger needs to be on your body rather than in your bag, when you want a loud physical siren, or when you would rather not depend on a phone at all.

An app wins on the thing that decides most real cases: you already have your phone, it is already charged, and you did not have to remember it.

The question that actually decides it

Not which is more capable. Which one will actually be on you at 11pm on a Tuesday when you were not expecting anything to happen.

That is the whole argument for the phone, and it is not a technical one. People do not leave the house without a phone. People absolutely do leave the house without the alarm they bought eight months ago, and most could not tell you whether its battery is alive.

Where a device genuinely wins

  • A loud physical siren. Attracting attention is a real strategy, and a phone speaker is not the same thing.
  • It survives your phone being taken. A standalone device keeps working; an app does not.
  • It is on your body, not in your bag. If the bag is what gets pulled away, that difference matters.

Questions people ask

Are safety devices better than safety apps?

Neither is better in general; they fail differently. A device fails when you left it at home or its battery died months ago unnoticed. An app fails if your phone is taken or flat. The practical question is which failure is more likely for you. For most people the phone is the thing they never leave without, which is why an app tends to be the more reliable default.

What about smart jewellery and keychain alarms?

The loud physical siren is a real advantage an app cannot match, and attracting nearby attention is a legitimate strategy on its own. Two things to check before buying: whether it works standalone or only as a Bluetooth button for a phone app, and how you would ever know its battery had died. A silent dead device is worse than no device, because you were relying on it.

Can I use both a device and SafeVaani?

Yes, and it is a reasonable belt-and-braces setup, particularly if the device has a loud siren. They cover different failure modes: the device covers a dead or stolen phone, and SafeVaani covers the day you left the device on the dresser.

Does SafeVaani work with Bluetooth panic buttons?

Not currently. SafeVaani triggers by voice, by manual SOS in the app, and through its own shortcuts. There is no integration with third-party Bluetooth buttons today.
