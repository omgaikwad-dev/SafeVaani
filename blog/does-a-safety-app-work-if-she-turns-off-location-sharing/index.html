Does a Safety App Still Work If She Turns Off Location Sharing?

In most apps, no. Her pin stays grey through the emergency. SafeVaani now releases a live location for as long as an SOS lasts, then closes it again.

Here is a question worth sitting with. A woman turns on location sharing for the walk home. Two hours pass and the window lapses, which is what she asked it to do. Later that night, something happens and she says the word.

Her mother opens the app.

In most safety apps, what she sees is a grey pin from nine o’clock. The sharing setting expired, so the map stopped updating, and it does not care that there is an emergency in progress.

That is the app having the feature and not the function.

An SOS now outranks a sharing setting

SafeVaani releases a live location to your circle whenever an SOS is active, whatever your sharing setting says and however old your last position was. Your card goes to the top of their list. It closes by itself the moment the SOS is resolved.

Two things it deliberately does not do.

It does not turn your sharing on. That would be a lie about what you agreed to, and it would outlive the emergency: you would come out the other side of the worst night of your life having silently opted into permanent live tracking. Your setting is exactly where you left it afterwards.

And it does not pretend to be ordinary sharing. Your circle is told the position is there because of an emergency, not because you chose to broadcast it.

An SOS is the request. A safety app whose location sharing is unavailable during the one event it was installed for is not a safety app.

A dead pin used to look exactly like a live one

This is the quieter problem, and it is worse than it sounds.

A map shows a dot. The dot from four minutes ago and the dot from four days ago are the same dot. Somebody looking at it has to work out the difference from a timestamp, and mostly they do not, because the pin looks alive.

Three things changed.

Age is now worked out on our side, not left to each screen to calculate. That sounds like a technical detail and it is not: it means no screen in the app can forget to ask, which is exactly what had happened.

A phone that stops reporting says so. If someone is sharing and nothing has arrived for 20 minutes, their card reads Not updating, and their circle gets a notification. Twenty minutes is deliberately not tighter. A phone in a pocket on a train misses fixes constantly, and an alert that fires every time somebody goes into a metro tunnel is an alert people switch off.

A dying phone warns the people who would notice. At 15 percent, once, your circle is told your location may stop updating soon. It says what it means for them rather than what the number is.

If your phone is charging, nobody is told anything. A phone at 11 percent next to a cable is not about to stop reporting, and an alert that fires for it teaches people to swipe alerts away. Once they learn that, they swipe away the real one.

Somewhere in this neighbourhood

Until now, sharing was all or nothing: an exact pin, or a grey dot and no updates. There was nothing in between.

You can now set your sharing to a street or a neighbourhood. Your circle sees a soft circle on the map instead of a pin, and their card says Approximate, within 2 km rather than pretending otherwise.

The part that matters is where this happens. The blurring is done before your position leaves our database. Your exact coordinates are never sent to the app at all, so there is nothing to intercept, nothing sitting in a cache, and nothing for us to hand over.

There is a version of this feature that looks identical and does not work. If an app adds a random wobble to your position on every refresh, anybody watching the map for an evening can average out the wobble and recover exactly where you are. The more often the map updates, the better that works, and the person doing it is somebody you added to your circle.

So SafeVaani does not add a wobble. It snaps your position to a fixed area, and the same true position always returns the same answer. Two hundred readings give one answer. There is nothing to average.

Your trail is reduced to the same precision, because a blurred pin over an exact breadcrumb trail hides nothing. And an SOS reports your true position regardless. A neighbourhood is no use to somebody trying to reach you.

Getting somewhere, and saying so

Two additions for ordinary evenings rather than emergencies.

Arrival alerts. SafeVaani has always been able to tell a circle when somebody left a place during the hours they were expected to be there. That is the alarm. It could never tell anyone when somebody arrived, which is the thing families actually read every day. It can now.

Getting this right is mostly about not sending it. Your phone reports arriving at a place it is already sitting in every time you open an app, a GPS fix drifting across a boundary produces arrivals from a phone on a table, and stepping out to the shop for four minutes is not a journey. An arrival only counts if you genuinely left first and stayed away for at least ten minutes.

Check in. One tap, and your circle knows you are fine. Your location goes with it only if you want it to. It exists because the only way to reassure people used to be leaving live sharing running all evening, which trades a permanent stream of your position for one sentence you wanted to send once.

Crash and fall detection, and what it cannot do yet

There is a new feature that watches for a hard impact followed by stillness, gives you a countdown, and alerts your circle if you do not respond. Your circle is told a sensor raised the alert rather than a person, because that changes what they should do: someone who pressed a button can usually answer their phone.

It is switched off by default, in Settings.

Two things we would rather say than let you assume.

It only watches while SafeVaani is open on your screen. It will not detect a crash with your phone in a pocket while you are driving. If you have used an American family tracker and are picturing that, this is not that yet.

And it is new. The thresholds that decide what counts as an impact are not yet tuned against Indian handsets. The countdown is your protection: if it fires when it should not, cancelling costs you five seconds and nobody is told. Turn it on, and tell us when it gets it wrong.

The through line

None of this is about knowing more about where somebody is. Most of it is about the app being honest about what it does and does not know.

A pin that is forty minutes old should not look live. A position you chose to blur should not say approximate while quietly sending the exact one. A battery warning should not fire for a phone on a charger. A crash detector that only works with the screen on should say so on the screen where you switch it on.

An app that overstates what it knows is worse than one that knows less, because somebody makes a decision on it.

Available in the latest SafeVaani build. Location precision and crash detection are in Settings. Arrival alerts are set per place when you create a safe zone.

Frequently asked questions

If someone turns off location sharing, can their circle still find them in an emergency?

In SafeVaani, yes, and only during the emergency. Location sharing is a bounded window: you turn it on for two hours and it lapses. If an SOS is raised after it lapses, SafeVaani releases a live location to your circle for exactly as long as that SOS is active, then closes it again automatically. It does not switch your sharing back on, and your setting is exactly where you left it afterwards.

Will my family be told if my phone battery is low?

Yes, once, at 15 percent, and only if you are sharing location with them. It is a quiet notification saying your location may stop updating soon, not an alarm. If your phone is on a charger, nobody is told at all, because a phone at 11 percent next to a cable is not about to go anywhere.

How do I know if someone's location is live or just the last place they were?

SafeVaani says so in words on their card. If a phone is sharing but has sent nothing for 20 minutes, it reads Not updating and their circle gets a notification. A pin from forty minutes ago used to look exactly like a pin from now, which is the single most misleading thing a map can do.

Can I share roughly where I am without sharing my exact location?

Yes. You can set your sharing precision to a street or a neighbourhood instead of exact. Your circle sees a circle rather than a pin, and the exact coordinates never leave our database at all. An active SOS overrides this and reports your true position, because a neighbourhood is not useful to somebody trying to reach you.

Does SafeVaani have crash detection?

There is a new crash and fall detection feature, it is switched off by default, and we are being straight about its limits. It only watches while SafeVaani is open on your screen, so it will not detect a crash with your phone in a pocket while driving. It is new and still being tuned. Turn it on and tell us if it fires when it should not.

How do I tell my family I got home without sharing my location all evening?

Check in. One tap posts a note to your circle, with your location if you want it attached and without it if you do not. It exists so you do not have to leave live sharing running all evening just to send one sentence.

How long does SafeVaani keep location history?

7 days, then it is deleted automatically. Not anonymised, not archived, deleted. If you have reduced your sharing precision, your history is stored at that same reduced precision, because a blurred pin over an exact trail hides nothing.
