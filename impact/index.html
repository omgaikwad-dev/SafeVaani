Impact

Measuring what matters most:
time, trust, and safe arrivals.

Every number on this page is something you can check by installing the app. None of it is a usage figure, because we do not have usage figures worth publishing yet, and this is the last page on the internet that should invent them.

What we measure

0 bytes

of her audio leave the phone

Speech is recognised on the device by a model that sits on the phone. There is no server in the listening path, so there is nothing to intercept, subpoena or sell.

0 bars

of signal needed to raise an alarm

With no network, the SOS hands off to nearby phones running SafeVaani and travels outward through them until one of them has a connection.

7 days

before a route deletes itself

Location history exists so somebody can retrace where she went. After a week it is gone, automatically, whether or not anyone asks.

2 languages

recognised on-device today

English ships inside the app. Hindi is a one time download you switch on, and after that both run offline. More are in training, and none of them will ship before they work in a noisy street.

What is deliberately not on this page

Response times. Alerts handled. Helpers on duty. Lives changed. We are early enough that any of those would be a rounding error dressed up as a result, and a safety company that inflates its first hundred numbers has already told you what it will do with its first hundred thousand. They go up here the week they are real, measured the same way every time, and not before.

How we change things

01

Zero-friction rescue

Instant voice activation

The worst minute of somebody’s life is not a minute in which to unlock a phone, find an app and press a button. SafeVaani listens for one word. It works with the screen off, the phone in a bag, and both hands busy.

Shipping today on Android.

02

Working when networks fail

Offline resilience

Dead zones are not an edge case in India, they are Tuesday. Basements, lifts, highways, power cuts, a jammed cell. An alarm that needs four bars is an alarm that fails exactly where it is needed most, so ours does not need them.

Relay shipping; range depends on how many phones are nearby.

03

The helper network

Community-powered verification

An alert that reaches nobody is a notification. SafeVaani Helpers is the other half: people who opt in, verify who they are, and agree to be sent the emergency nearest them, with arrival confirmed by a code only she can give.

In development. Nobody is being dispatched today, and we will not say otherwise.

Where it starts

Campus first, because that is where the walk home is.

A hostel gate at eleven at night, a shared auto to the station, a two kilometre stretch between the last lit building and the room. That walk is the same on every campus in the country, and it is the one SafeVaani is built around.

We are starting with the districts we can actually stand behind: local emergency numbers checked against official sources, transit guidance written for the people who make that walk, and no invented safety score for anybody’s neighbourhood.

Trust is the metric

A safety app that quietly becomes a location business has not failed its users slowly. It has failed them completely, on day one, in a way none of them can see.

Her audio is processed on her own phone and never uploaded. Her location is opt-in, visible to the people she picks, and erased after seven days. We do not sell any of it, to colleges, to advertisers, to anybody, at any price.

That is not a privacy policy talking. It is the reason the architecture looks the way it does: there is no server-side copy of her voice to sell, because we chose not to build one. The cheapest way to keep a promise is to make it impossible to break.
